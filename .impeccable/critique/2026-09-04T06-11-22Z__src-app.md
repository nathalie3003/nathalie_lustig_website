## Anti-Patterns Verdict

Not "AI made this". It reads as a real system with four un-audited reflexes still in it.

What clears it: blush genuinely reads as paper; hairline discipline is real; raspberry is rationed at 5.49:1 and used only at hairlines, labels, links and one button; the Treasury curve is live data with a real fallback, not a hero-metric template; the photographs are hers. The article header is publication-grade and would pass anywhere.

**Deterministic scan**: 11 CLI findings. 3 side-tab, 2 layout-transition, 2 design-system-radius, 3 design-system-color, 1 design-system-font. Browser overlay found 11 anti-patterns on the homepage, 17 on /notes, 3 on the article, 2 on About.

**Confirmed reflexes:**
- The rotating hero word, swapping every 2s forever, in the first viewport, directly under a standfirst about restraint.
- `.l-eyebrow` is sans 13px/600, not the mono department label DESIGN.md specifies. Three stack inside one 296px rail card.
- `.rail-accent` is a 3px gradient bar: a second accent rule on the same screen, which the DESIGN.md masthead rule explicitly forbids.
- Two 2-column grids each render an orphan: About Right now (5 cards) and Keep reading (often 1 card in a 2-col grid).

**False positives:** the 3 side-tab hits are pull-quote left rules, sanctioned in DESIGN.md and a legitimate typographic device rather than the card stripe the ban targets. The design-system-font hit on opengraph-image is Source Serif 4, which is documented. tight-leading at 1.20 on display headings is a body-text rule misapplied. The reply-hp overflow is the spam honeypot working as intended. Two design-system-color hits are the status dots, documented in DESIGN.md but not yet in the stale sidecar.

## Priority Issues

### P0 The reading column is 84 characters wide

`globals.css:1018` sets `.ap-col { max-width: var(--col-wide) }` = 900px, with body at 18.5px. Measured live: 836px content, 84 to 85 characters per line. DESIGN.md caps body at 65 to 75ch and specifies 19.5px. `--col-text: 680px` is defined and never used on prose.

This is the fourth design principle failing literally, on the page a recruiter opens from a shared link.

Fix: scope a narrow measure to prose children, keep `--col-wide` for breakout elements like the data strip and hero image.

### P0 `--ink-45` is 2.96:1 and colours 50 declarations including an interactive control

Measured, not estimated: 2.96:1 on blush, 2.95:1 on card. `--ink-60` is 4.73:1, `--ink-72` is 7.18:1.

It colours the article back link at 12.5px (an interactive control at 2.96:1), every note date and read time, the chart's axis values, sources citations, the trade disclaimer at 10.5px, reply timestamps, and the contact band labels over a photograph, which is worse than measured. PRODUCT.md commits to AA.

Also here: `.reply-post:disabled { opacity: 0.45 }` composites to 2.02:1, and that is the button's default state on every article.

Fix: retire `--ink-45` as a text colour, move text uses to `--ink-60`, keep it for hairlines and icons only.

### P1 No focus indicator anywhere on the site

Zero `:focus-visible` rules exist. `article.css:81` sets `outline: none` on the reply fields with only `:focus-within` on the parent form as a substitute, which is already on when tabbing between the two fields, so moving between them produces no visible change. WCAG 2.4.7 fail.

Compounding: the Notes dropdown opens on `onMouseEnter` only. Its trigger is a Link carrying `aria-expanded` and `aria-haspopup` that navigates on Enter, so ARIA promises a popup no key can open.

### P1 The curve card is mouse-only and its numbers render at 5px on mobile

At 375px the SVG scales to 0.563, so `.curve-tick` at 9px renders at 5.07px and `.curve-xlabel` at 5.35px. Every yield value and tenor is sub-6px. Separately the dot readout is `onMouseEnter` only, so on touch or keyboard there is no way to read any individual tenor.

This is the site's best credibility artefact and mobile is where a shared link is opened.

### P1 The recruiter never finds the candidate

PRODUCT.md weights recruiters and readers equally. The rail, About, the project stack and the contact band are all built for the reader. There is no CV, no byline on any article, and no statement of what she is looking for. `.l-btn-cv` is fully specified in globals.css and `.cv-btn` is a dead class name on a Get in touch button. A recruiter can read a whole note without learning whose it is.

## Persona Red Flags

**Capital-markets recruiter, 15 seconds**: hero and curve pass. Then the dominant homepage image is a holiday photo in a boat, and the sticky rail spends its space on coffee, Chopin and books. No CV, no byline. She will believe the writing is real; she will not find the candidate.

**Keyboard / screen-reader user**: Notes dropdown unreachable. No focus indicator site-wide. Chart readouts unreachable. Carousel captions announced every 4.5s via `aria-live="polite"`, unprompted and unstoppable.

**Mobile commuter**: 5px axis labels. The nav CTA vanishes at 760px leaving a 9-row hamburger with 34px rows. Body measure drops to 36ch. The contact band email overflows its column by 16px.

- Article body copy is `opacity: 0` for 1.2s gated on a JS class. With slow JS or a hydration hiccup the note ships with no title, deck or first paragraph.

- The Book Portfolio screenshot's baked-in title contradicts the card's own title.

## Questions to Consider

1. If a recruiter is genuinely 50% of the audience, what is the recruiter's version of the right rail, and why is the CV button DESIGN.md already specifies nowhere on the site?
2. DESIGN.md says 65 to 75ch and the reading surface ships 84ch. How many of those rules are load-bearing and how many are aspirational?
3. The taxonomy appears four times on one page load. If you kept one, which earns its place, and does the answer reveal the homepage does not need a filter for six rows?
4. The project stack costs two viewport-heights for a jewellery label and a book blog, sitting between the writing and the contact details. What is it proving that Recent writing has not already proved better?
